## Summary

This whole layer of the project (mocap data, humanoid MJCF models, retargeting) is almost entirely CPU/NumPy/MuJoCo work, so Apple Silicon is not a real obstacle — unlike the RL-training layer. I verified this by actually resolving the dependency sets with uv for Python 3.12 on this machine and by loading and benchmarking four candidate humanoid MJCFs in MuJoCo 3.11.0 locally. The single lowest-friction path to a walking gait is dm_control's bundled CMU mocap: a 454 MB HDF5 auto-downloaded from a public Google Cloud bucket with no registration, already retargeted to the 56-DoF `humanoid_CMU_V2020` walker, with curated `walk_tiny` / `locomotion_small` clip subsets and an Apache-2.0 tracking task. AMASS is the richer option and registration is instant self-service (no approval queue) across four separate MPI accounts, but everything MPI-licensed — AMASS, SMPL, SMPL-X, SMPL+H/MANO, and MyoLab's MyoSkeleton — is strictly non-commercial research use, and the SMPL+H model conversion step depends on `chumpy`, which is abandoned since 2020 and does not install or import on modern Python/NumPy without a specific workaround I verified locally. For models, the honest ranking on human realism is LocoMuJoCo SkeletonTorque (Hamner2010 OpenSim-derived, anatomical joint limits) ≈ dm_control humanoid_CMU (56 DoF, real joint limits) > SMPLSim `smpl_humanoid.xml` (69 DoF but every joint limit is ±180°, i.e. no anatomy) >> Gymnasium Humanoid (17 DoF, 42 kg, 1.57 m — a cartoon). For retargeting, GMR is the best-maintained and it is CPU-only by design, but every one of its 18 targets is a robot, so pointing it at a human-proportioned MJCF means authoring one JSON config yourself.

## Key facts

- MEASURED LOCALLY (M3 Max, macOS 26.5 arm64, MuJoCo 3.11.0, single-threaded mj_step x20000, 2026-08-12) — SMPLSim smpl_humanoid.xml: nq=76 nv=75 nu=69, 25 bodies, 25 geoms (16 capsule + 8 box), 71.8 kg, dt=0.002 → 11,760 steps/s = 23.5x realtime
- MEASURED LOCALLY — dm_control humanoid_CMU.xml: nq=63 nv=62 nu=56, 32 bodies, 50 geoms, 51.8 kg, 1.69 m tall, dt=0.002 → 16,150 steps/s = 32.3x realtime
- MEASURED LOCALLY — LocoMuJoCo skeleton_torque.xml: nq=38 nv=37 nu=31, 21 bodies, 80 geoms (STL bone meshes), 86.6 kg, dt=0.002 → 16,929 steps/s = 33.9x realtime. skeleton_muscle.xml is the same skeleton with nu=106 (92 muscle + 14 motor) → 12,076 steps/s = 24.2x realtime
- MEASURED LOCALLY — Gymnasium humanoid.xml: nq=24 nv=23 nu=17, 14 bodies, only 42.1 kg and 1.57 m, RK4/PGS dt=0.003 → 13,263 steps/s = 39.8x realtime. Mass and DoF count are not human — unusable as a 'realistic human-proportioned' target
- JOINT LIMITS (parsed from the XMLs directly, this is the single biggest differentiator): SMPLSim smpl_humanoid.xml gives EVERY joint range='-180 180' (elbows are '-720 720'). There are no anatomical limits at all — knees hyperextend, elbows spin. dm_control humanoid_CMU has real limits (ltibiarx knee 1°..170° flexion-only, lradiusrx elbow -10°..170°, lfemurrx hip -160°..20°). LocoMuJoCo skeleton_torque also has real limits (knee_angle_r -2.0944..0.174533 rad, elbow_flex_r -0.3..2.618 rad)
- SMPLSim smpl_humanoid.xml structure: SMPL's 24-body kinematic tree, freejoint + 23x3 Euler hinge triplets = 69 torque motors with gear='1' (PHC applies its own PD on top via the per-joint user='250 2.5 1 500 10 2' attribute). Per-body mass comes from geom density fitted to SMPL mesh volume (e.g. Pelvis density=3165.8). Default pose is SMPL's supine T-pose (measured z-extent 0.376 m) — you need the repo's `upright_start` option to get a standing humanoid
- LocoMuJoCo's SkeletonTorque provenance is stated in the XML header comment verbatim: 'Created by Guoping Zhao, Lauflabor, TU Darmstadt.' / 'Based on Hamner2010 OpenSim model.' / 'Simplify knee joint to a single DoF revolution joint.' — i.e. a real biomechanics model, not a hand-tuned RL toy

- GMR's ik_config JSON schema and its mink-based IK solver loop — the cleanest template for writing your own retargeter. - mink 1.2.0 directly, as the IK primitive — arm64 wheels, mujoco-native, tiny dependency footprint. If you write any retargeting or any waypoint/foot-placement IK for Phase 5, this is the library.
- SMPLSim's SMPL robot generator (smpl_local_robot.py) if you ever want a body-shape-parameterized humanoid — it emits MJCF for arbitrary SMPL betas and genders, capsule or mesh. Only useful if you accept the SMPL non-commercial license, and you would have to author the 69 joint limits yourself.
- MoCapAct's clip→snippet segmentation scheme (836 clips → 2589 snippets of 4-6 s with 1 s overlap) as a ready-made recipe for chopping reference motions into RSI-friendly training episodes. Free to copy even without downloading their 46 GB rollouts.
- 100STYLE (CC BY 4.0, commercial-safe, 1.5 GB BVH) as the Phase-3 gait-style variety source and as an insurance policy against the AMASS/LAFAN1 license problems. Its Frame_Cuts.csv and Dataset_List.csv save you the T-pose trimming work.
- The verified chumpy-on-Python-3.10 recipe in install_command — if you go the AMASS route, this unblocks the SMPL+H merge step that otherwise stops the pipeline dead.
- The four downloaded MJCFs and the bench.py harness already in the scratchpad — reuse them directly for the Phase-0 DESIGN.md model comparison rather than re-deriving the numbers.

## Blockers

- chumpy 0.70 (last release 2020-08-26) is a hard dependency of the SMPL / SMPL+H .pkl pipeline and is broken twice over on modern Python. VERIFIED LOCALLY: (a) `uv pip install chumpy` fails on BOTH Python 3.12 and 3.10 with 'ModuleNotFoundError: No module named pip' because its setup.py imports pip under build isolation; (b) once built it upgrades NumPy to 2.x and then fails at import with "ImportError: cannot import name 'int' from 'numpy'" because chumpy/__init__.py does `from numpy import bool, int, float, complex, object, unicode, str, nan, inf`, all removed in NumPy>=1.24. WORKAROUND I VERIFIED WORKS on this exact machine: a throwaway Python 3.10 venv, `uv pip install pip setuptools wheel`, then `uv pip install --no-build-isolation chumpy`, then force `numpy==1.22.4` and `scipy==1.10.1` back down AFTER chumpy is installed. Keep this env quarantined from the 3.12 training env.
- loco-mujoco on PyPI is 16 months stale (1.0.0, 2025-04-17, pinning mujoco==3.2.7 and numpy<2.0) while the current v1.1.0 is GitHub-only (tagged 2026-03-10, requires mujoco>=3.5.0 and numpy>2.0). Installing `pip install loco-mujoco` gets you the old one. You must install from git — and master's pyproject also declares `loco-mujoco-models` as a git dependency that does not exist on PyPI, so a purely-PyPI lockfile is impossible for this package.
- loco-mujoco v1.1.0 hard-depends on mujoco-warp>=3.5.0, whose backend warp-lang is CPU-only on Apple Silicon (Warp's GPU path needs a CUDA-capable NVIDIA GPU, minimum GTX 9xx). It will install (verified: warp_lang-1.16.0-py3-none-macosx_11_0_arm64.whl exists, and the whole loco-mujoco master dependency set resolves cleanly on Python 3.12 macOS arm64) but the MjWarp and MJX acceleration paths give you nothing here. Budget for CPU-only rollouts.
- LICENSE WALL — if this project is ever anything other than non-commercial research, AMASS, SMPL, SMPL-X, SMPL+H/MANO, DMPL and MyoLab MyoSkeleton are all off the table. That eliminates the SMPL-humanoid MJCF (which is a mean-shape SMPL body, so the SMPL non-commercial term arguably follows it even though SMPLSim's own code is BSD-3), the MyoSkeleton, and the entire AMASS corpus in one stroke. What survives commercially: CMU Graphics Lab mocap, 100STYLE (CC BY 4.0), dm_control's CMU HDF5 + humanoid_CMU walker, and LocoMuJoCo's SkeletonTorque model code (MIT) — though not its LAFAN1-derived HuggingFace data.
- LAFAN1 is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. The ND (NoDerivatives) clause makes retargeted LAFAN1 legally awkward regardless of commercial intent, and loco-mujoco's HuggingFace dataset propagates cc-by-nc-nd-4.0 to everything in it including the DefaultDatasets. PHUMA's authors independently excluded their LAFAN1 portion from public release for exactly this reason. Do not build the gait reference set on LAFAN1.
- Human3.6M requires the access request to come from an academic email address per its EULA clause 1. With feliks.torosyan@icloud.com this is a dead end, and the dataset is the wrong shape for physics imitation anyway.
- SMPLSim's smpl_humanoid.xml has NO anatomical joint limits — every hinge is range='-180 180' (elbows '-720 720'). A PPO/AMP policy trained on it will happily discover hyperextended knees and spinning elbows that look nothing like human gait, and the AMP discriminator will not necessarily punish it since the reference motions never visit those states. If you use this model you must author the joint limits yourself; that is real work on 69 hinges.
- The AMASS download page (download.php) returns HTTP 302 to login, so I could NOT verify the exact per-subset file sizes or the current full subset list from the primary source. My subset list comes from HumanML3D's preprocessing notebook and uses the older folder naming (BioMotionLab_NTroje, MPI_Limits, MPI_mosh) which AMASS has since renamed (BMLrub, PosePrior, MoSh). Verify against the live page after you register. I deliberately did not create an account.
- KIT's bulk export is unavailable ('The API is currently deactivated', exports 'currently working on') so there is no scriptable way to pull its 2972 motions. Treat KIT as reachable only through the AMASS KIT subset.
- GMR ships zero human-proportioned targets — all 18 ik_configs map SMPL-X or BVH onto commercial robots. Nothing in it retargets onto a human skeleton out of the box.
- MimicKit, the reference AMP/DeepMimic implementation, only supports Isaac Gym, Isaac Lab, and Newton. The first two are CUDA-only and therefore disqualified on this machine.
- The CMU mocap site (mocap.cs.cmu.edu) presents a TLS certificate that fails verification — WebFetch and any HTTPS-upgrading client will error with 'unable to verify the first certificate'. Downloads must go over plain http:// or with certificate verification disabled, which also means the 1 GB archive arrives unauthenticated.

## Performance evidence

- MEASURED ON THE TARGET MACHINE, not published numbers. M3 Max, macOS 26.5 (25F71), arm64, Python 3.12, mujoco 3.11.0, single-threaded mj_step loop of 20,000 steps from qpos0 (humanoid falls and makes ground contact during the run, so these include contact solving). Script at /private/tmp/claude-502/-Users-BrickLayer-Desktop-HumonoidAI/24c51158-3311-4d49-a1c8-8e6bff1681b1/scratchpad/bench.py
- LocoMuJoCo SkeletonTorque (nv=37, nu=31, 80 mesh geoms, 86.6 kg, dt=0.002): 16,929 steps/s = 33.9x realtime
- dm_control humanoid_CMU (nv=62, nu=56, 50 geoms, 51.8 kg, dt=0.002): 16,150 steps/s = 32.3x realtime
- Gymnasium Humanoid (nv=23, nu=17, 18 geoms, 42.1 kg, RK4 dt=0.003): 13,263 steps/s = 39.8x realtime
- LocoMuJoCo SkeletonMuscle (nv=37, nu=106 = 92 muscle + 14 motor, dt=0.002): 12,076 steps/s = 24.2x realtime — muscle actuation costs ~29% throughput versus the torque version of the identical skeleton
- SMPLSim smpl_humanoid (nv=75, nu=69, 24 capsule/box geoms, 71.8 kg, dt=0.002): 11,760 steps/s = 23.5x realtime — the slowest of the four despite having the fewest geoms, because of the 69-DoF tree
- Practical read: on one core you get ~12-17k physics steps/s. With a 30 Hz control rate and frame-skip 4 (dt=0.002 → 8 ms per control step) that is roughly 3,000-4,200 env-steps/s/core. Across 10 performance cores with vectorized/subprocess envs you should budget on the order of 30-40k env-steps/s before Python and policy-inference overhead, which is the real ceiling for PPO wall-clock here.
- No published Apple Silicon throughput benchmarks exist for any of loco-mujoco, GMR, SMPLSim, or MyoSuite. I searched their GitHub issue trackers for macos/mac/apple/arm64/metal and every query returned 'No results' — these are Linux-first research repos. Absence of bug reports is not evidence of support; it is evidence that nobody has tried.
- GMR's own claim (README, not independently verified here) is 35-70 FPS retargeting on CPU. Since retargeting is a one-time offline preprocessing pass over a few hours of mocap, even an order of magnitude slower would be irrelevant to this project.
